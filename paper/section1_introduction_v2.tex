\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Bayesian models with finite exchangeability---mixture models,
hidden Markov models, factor models with interchangeable
components---define posterior distributions that are invariant
under permutation of component labels.  This symmetry creates
$m!$~equivalent posterior modes, a phenomenon known as \emph{label
switching} \citep{Stephens2000,Jasra2005,Celeux2000}.
The standard response is to run MCMC on the full labelled
parameterisation and then relabel or reorder the samples post~hoc
\citep{FruhwirthSchnatter2001,Papastamoulis2010}.
But the underlying chain must still traverse $m!$~symmetric modes,
and convergence diagnostics computed on this redundant space are
unreliable: certified spectral-gap bounds, when they can be
computed at all, are inflated by cross-mode oscillation and
frequently vacuous.

A cleaner alternative is to perform inference directly on the
\emph{quotient posterior}: the distribution on equivalence classes
induced by the symmetry group.  By sampling on a fundamental
domain---a sorted chamber, a reflection half-space---the chain
never encounters label switching, and the inference target is the
identifiable object of interest.  This idea has appeared informally
in the mixture-model literature
\citep{Celeux2000,FruhwirthSchnatter2001}, but has not been
combined with transport-map proposals or convergence certification.

This paper introduces \emph{Folded Transport MCMC} (FolT-MCMC),
a quotient-space independence sampler whose proposal density is
constructed by symmetrising a learned normalising flow over the
orbits of the symmetry group.  The method pairs naturally with the
LCNF oscillation-based certification framework
\citep{LCNF,CerTMCMC}: folding onto the fundamental domain
removes the cross-mode oscillation that makes unfolded certificates
vacuous, while a quotient-metric covering argument preserves the
ball-mass bounds needed for certification.

The three main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Quotient posterior framework.}
  For a target~$\pi$ invariant under a finite group~$G$ ($|G|=s$),
  we define the folded target $\pi_F = s \cdot \pi$ on the
  fundamental domain~$D$, prove an exact HPD identity
  $\cK_\alpha^F = \cK_\alpha \cap D$, and construct the
  quotient proposal $q_F(z) = \sum_{g \in G} q_{T_F}(g \cdot z)$.
  The density floor improves by a factor of~$s$
  (Section~\ref{sec:framework}).

  \item \textbf{Quotient-space certification.}
  We show that the LCNF empirical oscillation theorem transfers
  to the quotient metric with a stabiliser-corrected ball-mass
  bound, that the covering radius does not increase
  ($\varepsilon_F^* \leq \varepsilon_U^*$), and that the
  quantile-core certified lower bound
  $\mathrm{QC}\,\underline{\gamma}$ improves whenever the unfolded
  flow exhibits cross-mode proposal deficiency
  (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).

  \item \textbf{Empirical validation.}
  On symmetric Gaussian mixtures ($d = 2$--$20$),
  label-switching targets ($m! = 2$--$24$ modes), and a standard
  Bayesian three-component mixture posterior, the
  QC~$\underline{\gamma}$ ratio ranges from $2\times$ to
  $145\times$, with the folded certificate empirically nearly
  dimension-free.  On real accelerometer data from a supertall
  building during Typhoon Mangkhut, FolT-MCMC yields a
  non-vacuous quantile-core certificate for a simplified
  closely-spaced three-mode identification problem, where the
  unfolded certificate is vacuous
  (Sections~\ref{sec:experiments}--\ref{sec:application}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
The quantile-core certificate is a computable diagnostic for
density-ratio stability on the high-probability posterior core; it
does not constitute a full-support spectral-gap guarantee, which
would require additional tail-control assumptions
(Section~\ref{sec:when_folding_helps}).  This limitation is shared
with the LCNF and CerT-MCMC frameworks on which FolT-MCMC builds.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:background} reviews transport MCMC, the LCNF
certification framework, and symmetry in Bayesian inference.
Section~\ref{sec:framework} develops the FolT-MCMC framework.
Section~\ref{sec:theory} establishes the theoretical guarantees.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} presents the synthetic and standard
Bayesian mixture experiments.
Section~\ref{sec:application} applies FolT-MCMC to structural
modal identification with real typhoon data.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses limitations and extensions.
